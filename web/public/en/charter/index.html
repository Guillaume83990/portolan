A typical week

From Saint‑Tropez to Monaco, in seven ports of call

An indicative route: the captain adjusts it every morning to the wind, your wishes and the rhythm of your guests.

  1. 01Saturday

    Saint‑Tropez, then Pampelonne

    Embarkation in the old port in the late afternoon. First anchorage in the bay of Pampelonne, dinner on board.

  2. 02Sunday

    Porquerolles

    The Plage d'Argent, the pines, water you would swear was tropical. Lunch on board, the afternoon cycling across the island.

  3. 03Monday

    Port‑Cros

    The national park and its underwater trail. The calmest day of the week.

  4. 04Tuesday

    L'Estérel

    Coves of red porphyry. Anchored at the foot of the rocks, swimming from the swim platform.

  5. 05Wednesday

    The Lérins islands

    Sainte‑Marguerite and Saint‑Honorat, the monastery and its vines. In the evening, Cannes is a tender ride away.

  6. 06Thursday

    Monaco

    Arrival in Port Hercule at blue hour. Dinner on board facing the illuminated Rock.

  7. 07Friday

    Back to Saint‑Tropez

    Along the coast, one last stop at Pampelonne. A final dinner facing the village.

Frequently asked questions

Before you step aboard

Do I need a boating licence?

No. The yacht is run by her captain and crew, who know every anchorage on the coast.

Can I embark somewhere other than Saint‑Tropez?

Yes: Monaco, Cannes, Antibes or Saint‑Raphaël. Delivery fees apply depending on the distance between the yacht's home port and your port of embarkation.

How far ahead should I book?

For July and August, from the previous autumn or winter: the best yachts go quickly. May, June and September remain more flexible, and are often more beautiful.

Can I charter for less than a week?

Yes, outside high season, from three days depending on the yacht. The smaller ones can also be chartered by the day.

Are children welcome?

Of course. We let the crew know, who provide suitable life jackets and, on request, a nanny on board.

What if the weather turns?

The captain adapts the route every morning to the wind. The coast always offers shelter: the week moves, it is never lost.

What is the advance provisioning allowance (APA)?

Around 30 % of the rate, paid before embarkation for fuel, food, drinks and berths. The captain keeps a daily account and refunds the balance.
